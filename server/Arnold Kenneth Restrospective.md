# 2. One real cross-team issue

A cross-team issue that happened was in Week 9 when I was serving as DevOps and helping with testing our endpoints with our downstream partner. Our partner team was calling our workers endpoint to filter available casual workers by status, but their integration kept breaking whenever a filter returned no matching workers. Instead of returning a 200 OK with an empty list, one of our routes was returning a 404 Not Found with an error string, and when their client automatically retried the failed request in a loop, it triggered our Week 9 rate limiter and started returning 429, too many requests.
We found out when their tester messaged us saying our API was blocking their IP during demo rehearsals. I checked our logs and our notes in CONTRACT_DEVIATIONS.md and saw the burst of 404s followed immediately by 429 status codes. Wahome, Kwezikulu, and I sat down at Kwezi’s laptop, changed the query handler so that an empty filter result returned to a standard 200 status, and adjusted the rate limit window so normal integration testing would not trigger it. We logged the change in INTEGRATION_LOG.md and VERSIONING_NOTES.md, pushed the fix, and confirmed with our partner on a live call that their app handled the empty state cleanly. The whole fix took about two hours from their first message to green tests on both sides.

# 3. What I would design differently

If I could start  semester again, I would change two specific things from the beginning. First, I would standardize all of our database IDs as strings in Week 2 instead of mixing numbers and strings, because json-server compatibility issues cost us hours of debugging when we rotated into the final backend polish in Weeks 11 to 14. Second, I would enforce a rule that no endpoint is considered finished until its npx jest test is written and passing on a fresh clone, rather than letting implementation get ahead of testing during our group sessions on Kwezi’s laptop.
What I would keep is the blind testing exercise recorded in BLIND_TEST_REPORT.md and the role rotation itself. Even though rotating from QA to API Lead, DevOps, and finally Backend Developer wasn’t as convenient as I liked, it stopped us from working in silos. By Week 14, every person on the team understood how .env variables, rate limiting, and partner fallbacks connected because each of us had been responsible for a different piece of the system.
